## Where the $399 Figure Actually Comes From

This isn't one story. It's a few different things that all point back to roughly the same number, which is why the search is so persistent.

**It might just be the current price.** As covered above, the Jetson Bolt Pro Max is genuinely $399.99 on Costco.com today. If you searched and found conflicting info, it's likely because that listing changes availability by region and sometimes bounces in and out of stock.

**It could be an older Jetson model from 2022.** Costco has sold a version of the Jetson Bolt Pro folding bike for years, and the price has moved around quite a bit. Back in 2022, shoppers on deal forums reported grabbing it for $329.99 in store, down from a listed $359.99 online, with some locations pricing it as low as $269.99 during clearance events. One buyer even noted the retail price on that model usually ran around $599. None of those numbers are exactly $399, but they're close enough that memories blur, especially years later.

**It could be a rounded number from an ad you half remember.** Deal aggregator sites and warehouse fan blogs have posted Jetson folding e-bikes at $399.99 going back to at least early 2022. If you saw a similar post scrolling through Facebook or a coupon site, that image probably stuck with you longer than the actual date did.

**It's almost certainly not a scam ad for $89 or less.** If you've seen anything advertising an electric bike from Costco for under $100, that's not a real listing. A new adult electric bike under $100 isn't realistic, and ultra-cheap "Costco clearance" ads circulating on Facebook for numbers like $89 are scams. More on spotting those in a minute.

## What You Actually Get for Under $400

Let's be honest about what $399.99 buys you, because it's not the same bike as the $1,400 commuter models sitting a few rows over in the catalog.

The Jetson Bolt Pro Max is a compact, folding e-bike built for short trips, not long commutes. The 350 watt motor and 15.5 mph top speed put it well below the power of most Class 2 or Class 3 e-bikes you'd see at a specialty shop. The 220 pound weight capacity is on the lower end too, so it's worth checking that number carefully if you're a larger rider.

What it does have going for it is genuine portability. Folding frames and small wheels mean it fits in a trunk or a closet in a way a full-size commuter bike never will. Reviewers who've actually bought and ridden it describe assembly as simple and the ride itself as fun for what it is, especially for short errands or getting around a neighborhood.

That said, it's not without complaints. Some buyers reported receiving the occasional lemon, with components like brakes coming loose while riding, which is a real safety concern worth taking seriously before you hop on. The upside is that Costco's return policy gives you a safety net most other retailers don't if something does go wrong out of the box.

## How to Find Current Clearance and Warehouse-Specific Deals

Costco's online pricing is only part of the picture. In-warehouse pricing can run lower, and it varies by location, which is part of why the internet is full of slightly different numbers for the same bike.

A few practical ways to track down the actual current deal near you:

- **Check Costco.com directly** and filter to electric bikes. Online prices include shipping, so in-store can sometimes beat it.
- **Call your local warehouse** and ask what's in stock. E-bikes move in and out of physical inventory faster than the online catalog updates.
- **Watch deal forums** like Slickdeals, where members regularly post in-store markdowns with the specific warehouse location and dates the sale runs.
- **Sign up for Costco's email alerts**, since seasonal clearance on sporting goods and bikes tends to show up there before it hits general search results.
- **Ask about the Costco Fan Blog network**, an independent site (not run by Costco) that tracks regional flyer pricing, especially useful in Canada where warehouse pricing can differ from the national online catalog.

Keep in mind that a great warehouse deal at one location doesn't mean it exists everywhere. Inventory is genuinely regional, and a bike marked down in one state can be full price three states over.

## Red Flags at Ultra-Low Price Points

Here's where it's worth slowing down. Not everything wearing a Costco logo online is actually Costco.

Watch out for these signs before you buy anything advertised as a rock-bottom Costco e-bike deal:

- **Prices under $150 or so for a full-size adult e-bike.** That's below what any legitimate retailer can sell a real e-bike for, and it's a common bait tactic.
- **Links that don't go to costco.com or costco.ca.** Scam ads often route through lookalike domains or third-party marketplaces dressed up with Costco branding.
- **Ads on social media with countdown timers and urgent language.** Real Costco deals rarely need to pressure you into clicking in the next ten minutes.
- **No item number or model name listed.** Legitimate Costco listings always include a specific product, brand, and item number you can search directly on their site.
- **Requests for payment outside Costco's own checkout.** If you're asked to pay through a wire transfer, gift card, or a site that isn't Costco's actual domain, walk away.
